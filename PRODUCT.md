# Product

## Register

product

## Users

AQuantLens US/options is used by a local research operator who is evaluating U.S. equities, indices, liquid options, and paper-only strategy workflows. The user works in a focused desktop research context, switching between AI-generated Chinese-first reports, market data status, option-chain views, Strategy Lab candidates, and paper workflow review.

## Product Purpose

The product is a Chinese-first AI trading research workbench for U.S. equities, SPX/SPY/QQQ, selected liquid U.S. options, and paper-only validation. Success means the interface helps the operator inspect data readiness, run and compare research, review strategy candidates, understand paper risk, and advance paper workflows without implying live execution authority.

## Brand Personality

Precise, calm, institution-grade. The interface should feel like a serious internal research terminal with clear decision boundaries: dense enough for expert work, restrained enough to avoid distraction, and explicit whenever a workflow is paper-only or blocked by risk controls.

## Anti-references

Do not make this feel like a public trading app, broker execution console, live-order ticket, or promotional SaaS dashboard. Avoid fake static dashboard data, decorative finance theatrics, generic AI product gloss, and any UI language that suggests live trading, broker credentials, automatic execution, or paper-to-live promotion. No additional specific external anti-references are currently specified.

## Design Principles

1. Make paper-only boundaries unmistakable.
2. Put research evidence before visual drama.
3. Optimize for repeat expert workflows, not first-impression spectacle.
4. Prefer real runtime state and clear blocked states over mock optimism.
5. Keep bilingual surfaces operational, with Chinese-first reports and precise English market terminology where needed.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Baseline target is WCAG AA for contrast and interaction states. Motion should respect reduced-motion preferences. Market direction, risk, readiness, approval, rejection, and error states should not rely on color alone; use labels, icons, and structure so color-blind users can read the same status information.
